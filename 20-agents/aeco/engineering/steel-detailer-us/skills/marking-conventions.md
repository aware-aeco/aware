# Marking conventions (AISC)

AISC 303 builds marking on the **shipping-piece** concept: shop drawings are "drawings of the individual structural steel shipping pieces"; erection drawings show "the location and attachment of the **individual** shipping pieces"; and the bill of materials lists each item by **quantity, mark, description, length, and material grade**.

- **Erection marks** are physically applied to members: *"erection marks shall be applied to the structural steel members by painting or other suitable means."* (§6.5.1)
- **Drawing-number persistence:** each shop and erection drawing keeps the **same drawing number throughout the project**, with a revision number and date. (§4.2.3)
- **AESS:** fabrication and erection marks **must not be visible** for AESS Categories 2–4; mill marks not visible for AESS 3–4 (with exceptions for reused / weathering / galvanized / uncoated steel). (§10.4.2)

**Where mark *uniqueness* actually lives:** AISC 303 does **not** state an explicit "every piece mark must be unique" rule — uniqueness is *implicit* in the per-shipping-piece definitions and the bill-of-materials "mark" field. The detailed marking and uniqueness conventions are governed by **AISC 360 §M1**, the **AISC Detailing Manual**, and **NISD** detailing practice — cite those (not AISC 303) for a uniqueness rule.
